**How memories form.** Vex cannot write into long-term memory directly. Mid-conversation it can only suggest a lesson, and every suggestion is checked at the door: a recognized secret (seed words, a labelled hex private key, an API key with a known prefix, a JWT) rejects the whole suggestion, wallet addresses are masked, and a suggestion that is mostly live figures such as balances and prices is refused, because Vex records lessons, not snapshots. What passes becomes a pending candidate (see Memory candidates below). A background reviewer then applies fixed rules first, with no AI: duplicates and near-duplicates of an existing memory are recorded as duplicates and count as a confirmation of it, low-importance or one-off observations are kept but not promoted, and live-state text, evidence from a deleted session, or very low confidence with no evidence is rejected. The rest go to an AI judge, which scores grounding, durability, novelty, generality, and whether the lesson is about the decision process rather than a lucky outcome. Only a valid verdict of promote (or supersede, which replaces an older conflicting lesson) creates a long-term memory; a broken or invalid judge response creates nothing and the item is retried. Every verdict the reviewer reaches is logged in Manager decisions; suggestions rejected at the door and failed judge calls are not.

**How memories age.** Vex never deletes a memory. A new lesson starts on probation and ranks a little lower in search. Trade, strategy and risk lessons lose half their weight about every 30 days unless they are confirmed again, and they can fade faster when the market regime no longer matches; a faded lesson stays in the database and in search, and returns when a similar lesson is confirmed. Other kinds of lesson, such as a preference, do not fade.

**Trades and memory.** Lessons are not re-checked when a trade closes. The outcome re-check was retired, so a trade-related lesson is judged once, and its trade evidence is used only to confirm that the trade exists and to cap how far the lesson is trusted. Treat a trade-derived lesson as Vex's read at the time, not a verified outcome. Past outcomes, win or loss, are never a promise about future trades.

One habit still matters: never paste seed phrases or private keys into chat. Redaction recognizes known shapes only, so it is a safety net, not a reason to test it.
